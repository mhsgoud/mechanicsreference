The full PBC set

A periodic RVE is not loaded by gripping every boundary node to ε̄ x. Partner points on opposite sides are tied by a jump equal to the macro strain times the lattice vector. Faces first, then edges, then vertices — that is the full set. One independent ε̄ case at a time; face reactions fill one column of C̄.

uiP⁺ − uiP⁻ − ε̄ij (xjP⁺ − xjP⁻) = 0

Constrained FEM on the cell

The partner jumps in the figure are not Neumann loads and not a grip of every boundary node to ε̄ x. They are linear multi-point constraints on the discrete displacement vector:

C u = g,    with   u⁺ − u⁻ = ε̄ (x⁺ − x⁻)

Each face, edge, and vertex pair contributes rows of C; the right-hand side g carries the macro strain times the lattice vector. One independent ε̄ case fills one column of C̄ from the reactions on those constraints (or from a volume average of stress).

Three algebraic routes enforce the same rows: master–slave elimination (reduce to an unconstrained SPD system), Lagrange multipliers (exact constraints, indefinite saddle point, reactions as λ), or penalty / augmented Lagrangian (stay primal, approximate the jump). Over-constraining corners — a closed master–slave loop — is the usual implementation bug; keep one reference vertex free of that cycle.

The same machinery covers ordinary Dirichlet supports, rigid ties, and mortar continuity. See Constrained FEM for the discrete catalogue, and keep this page for the periodic kinematics that feed g.
