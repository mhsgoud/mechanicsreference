Governing equations

This layer sits under the encyclopedia overview. Continuum mechanics writes these as fields; finite elements and numerical methods approximate them on a mesh. Each identity also has a hub entry.

Continuum mechanics

Kinematics, stress, and conservation—the field problem before discretization.

Kinematics

Deformation gradient

F = ∂x / ∂X

Maps a material line element from the reference placement X to the current placement x.

Kinematics

Green–Lagrange strain

E = ½ (Fᵀ F − I)

Finite strain on the reference configuration. Reduces to infinitesimal strain for small displacement gradients.

Kinematics

Infinitesimal strain

ε = ½ (∇u + (∇u)ᵀ)

Symmetric gradient of displacement. The working strain in linear solid mechanics and linear FEM.

Stress

Cauchy traction

t = σ n

Force per current area. Angular momentum (no couple stresses) requires σ = σᵀ.

Balance

Conservation of mass

∂ρ/∂t + ∇ · (ρ v) = 0

Local mass balance. Incompressibility is ∇ · v = 0.

Balance

Linear momentum

∇ · σ + ρ b = ρ a

Cauchy’s first law. At rest, ∇ · σ + ρ b = 0.

Constitutive

Hooke’s law (isotropic)

σ = λ tr(ε) I + 2μ ε

Linear isotropic elasticity. Equivalently σ = ℂ : ε.

Fluids

Navier–Stokes (incompressible)

ρ (∂v/∂t + v · ∇v) = −∇p + μ ∇²v + ρ b

Same momentum balance, different constitutive response, with ∇ · v = 0.

Finite elements

The weak form and the discrete system that replace the strong field equations on a mesh.

Weak form

Principle of virtual work

∫ σ : ∇δu dV = ∫ t · δu dA + ∫ ρ b · δu dV

Weak statement of momentum. FEM interpolates u and δu with shape functions.

Discrete

Linear system

K a = f

After uʰ = N a and assembly: stiffness times nodal unknowns equals the load vector.

Numerical methods

How a nonlinear residual is turned into a sequence of linear solves.

Nonlinear solve

Newton–Raphson

KT Δu = −R(u)

Linearization of R(u) = 0. KT is the consistent tangent of the residual.
